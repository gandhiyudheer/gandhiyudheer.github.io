About

Started out as a mechanical engineer in Bengaluru. Fell for the way structures move under dynamic flow, enough to chase it into an aerospace MSc, and into the state-of-the-art vision-based navigation work behind my Coventry thesis. Then came a sharp turn into the numerical modelling of composites, the one that really stuck. It grew into a long research thread through Italy, and it's what brought me to Tokyo, building the tools other engineers get to use.

My PhD at the University of Bologna had a long title, but it came down to one question: given a 3D printer that lays down continuous fiber, what's the best way to route the fibers? I built methods to answer that, published in Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering, and they've been used on real parts, including components for the Ferrari 296GTB and the Emilia V solar car that won the Sasol 2024 Cruiser Challenge.

At Braid Technologies in Tokyo, I build the differentiable finite-element engine underneath AURA, Braid's system for going from design intent to manufacturable geometry instead of the manual draw-and-simulate loop. In practice that is a lot of finite element analysis, linear and nonlinear, element formulations including shells, gradient-based optimization with adjoint sensitivities, and the verification work that decides whether any of it can be trusted.

One idea ties it all together for me: geometry. How you describe a shape decides what you can do with it. The fiber-routing methods, the tools that make a CAD model and its analysis model the same object, even the way nonlinear FEA handles rotations, they all come back to geometry. The longer I do this, the more the interesting problems look geometric underneath.

What keeps me going: seeing research end up in real cars, building tools that outlast the project they were made for, and the honest truth that most weeks are really just about fixing whatever broke.

The through-line

It usually comes down to geometry

The longer I work in computational mechanics, the more geometry seems to be running things underneath. The composite methods worked because the design choices were really about shape. Isogeometric analysis lets a CAD model and its analysis model be the same object. Even the rotation handling in nonlinear FEA is geometry. Most of my reading and side projects right now follow this thread.

NURBS Isogeometric analysis Geometry projection CAD-FE integration
Method

Isogeometric analysis & NURBS

Learning isogeometric analysis from the ground up, knot vectors, basis functions, refinement, and working out where it really beats classical finite elements on geometry-heavy problems.

Method

Nonlinear shell mechanics

How thin shells behave once things turn nonlinear, and how to model shells that buckle and touch without the solver falling over.

Foundations

The math under finite elements

Going back over the math that finite elements rest on, not for the proofs, but to see which modeling shortcuts quietly cost you accuracy.

Build

Simulation infrastructure

Shipping the building blocks at Braid: element formulations, nonlinear statics, contact, constraints, optimization sensitivities, mesh pipelines, and the verification that keeps them honest. The part I like most is when small, well-made pieces just get used, without anyone having to think about them.

Work

A path that kept bending toward harder problems.

  1. Aug 2025 – Now Tokyo, Japan

    Senior Researcher · Braid Technologies

    Building the finite-element engine underneath AURA, Braid's system for going from design intent to manufacturable geometry in place of the manual draw-and-simulate loop. Day to day: solid and shell element formulations (Mindlin and thin shell, corotational and isogeometric Kirchhoff-Love shells), nonlinear statics, contact and constraints, and topology and shape optimization with adjoint and automatic-differentiation sensitivities. I own gradient correctness and verification across the engine: Taylor remainder and dot product tests, V&V plans and analytical benchmark suites, and solid versus shell cross validation. Modal work too, from modal superposition and Galerkin projection through to per mode stress recovery. Commercial and open-source solvers (LS-DYNA, Kratos Multiphysics, MFEM) brought into one engine engineers actually want to use.

    Nonlinear FEA Contact · buckling Element formulations Topology & shape optimization Adjoint sensitivities Verification & validation Modal dynamics LS-DYNA / Kratos / MFEM Python · MATLAB
  2. Jan 2024 – Jun 2025 Forlì, Italy

    Postdoctoral Researcher · University of Bologna

    Took design-optimization research and made it work on real, additively-manufactured production parts.

    • Developed topology- and stress-based design-optimization methods for additively manufactured structural parts, from formulation to production hardware.
    • Co-led design, analysis, and 3D printing of parts for the Emilia V solar vehicle, winner of the Sasol 2024 Cruiser Challenge.
    • Built a stress-based design framework for 3D-printed Ferrari 296GTB mechanism parts, measurably lighter and stiffer, validated against test data.
    Design optimization Additive manufacturing Ferrari · Emilia collaborations

    Research Associate · University of Parma

    My first deep dive into computational composites. I modeled how shape-memory alloys respond to heat and stress (UMAT subroutines in Abaqus), worked out fiber layouts for bistable laminates, and modeled how composites start to delaminate and come apart.

    Shape memory alloys Bistable laminates Delamination · fracture Abaqus UMAT Fortran

    A detour into robotics that quietly changed how I think about optimization. I wrote path-planning and obstacle-avoidance algorithms (A* and D*) for a rover, and built a nonlinear controller, using particle swarm optimization, to keep it on track.
